The Lore

Why this place exists

Last updated: August 20, 2026

Somewhere along the way, "make a spreadsheet of AI tools" turned into "build a whole database, give it a scoring system, add a graveyard for the ones that died, wire up a live news feed, draw fantasy-tavern art for basically everything, and then attach a choose-your-own-adventure game with dice checks and a final boss." Nobody asked for that last part. We built it anyway. That's basically the whole origin story: this is what happens when cataloguing 226+ AI tools is treated less like a spreadsheet and more like a small kingdom that needs mapping.

The internet already has approximately one million "best AI tools" lists, most of them the same 30 tools copy-pasted into a different font by someone who has never actually opened the product. Solos Gems exists to be the one that isn't that: real testing, real pricing, real opinions about who a tool is a bad fit for, because "who shouldn't buy this" is the part most review sites conveniently skip. Then it grew a dragon problem and got a lot more fun to browse.

How reviews are built

Pricing and feature details are checked against current vendor documentation at the time of writing. Vendors change pricing often, so always confirm current cost on the vendor's own site before subscribing. Where a review includes hands-on impressions, screenshots, workflow notes, time-on-task, that's marked explicitly and is based on direct use of the tool, not generated from marketing copy.

How we rank

Every reviewed tool gets a score out of 10, weighted across four things: value relative to price, real-world capability, ease of use and setup friction, and how safely you can trial it, a generous free tier scores better than "no free plan, pay first." Nothing on this site scores a 10; if a tool were actually flawless, it wouldn't need a review, it would need a headline.

The overall ranking (#1–47, shown on the homepage and at the top of every review) is a single cross-category list, not a per-category leaderboard, so a design tool can outrank a bookkeeping tool if it delivers more value for the price. When a new review is added or an existing score is revised, every tool's position is recalculated and marked with how many spots it moved up or down since the last update. The current #1 gets a gem badge next to its name, which is the closest thing this site has to a trophy case.

Affiliate disclosure

Some links on this site are affiliate links, meaning we may earn a commission if you sign up through them, at no extra cost to you. This never affects which tools get covered or what the verdict says, a tool with no affiliate program gets the same treatment as one that pays 30% recurring.

What else lives on this site

Reviews are the main event, but a spreadsheet of scores gets old fast, so a few other things happen here too:

The homepage is a filterable, searchable wall of 226+ AI tools, search by name or filter by category, no scrolling through a wiki required. Hover any tool's logo and you'll get a small illustrated scene and a one-line joke about it, drawn in the same low-poly, tavern-and-torchlight style as everything else here, a genuinely unnecessary amount of effort spent making a database more fun to browse. There's also a live headline ticker under the top banner pulling real AI news, which occasionally scrolls past a story about a tool we've reviewed, so you can watch the ecosystem eat itself in near-real time.

DeathStack is the graveyard: 25 real AI tools and services that shut down, with what killed them and an interesting fact, narrated like a horror movie because betting your business on a single AI vendor is, frankly, a horror movie. News is that same live feed as its own page, auto-pulled from actual outlets, tagged for controversy and for anything that mentions a tool we cover, no editor cherry-picking headlines.

And then there's the quest, a small choose-your-own-adventure sitting right next to the logo in the header, because a database alone wasn't quite enough of a good time. Roll dice, dodge a toll troll, dodge an escalating gauntlet of obstacles in a couple of Temple-Run-style lane minigames, collect real reviewed tools as items you activate whenever you want, dig around for hidden choices most people never find, including a small overgrown graveyard where the ghosts of actual dead AI products hang around complaining, and try to reach Solos Gems before a golem talks you to death. It takes the review methodology above and turns it into a video game bit, which felt like the correct amount of overkill. Submit is the more sensible corner of the site, where you tell us about a tool we've missed or nominate the next entry for the graveyard.
