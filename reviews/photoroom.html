Photoroom is an AI photo editor built specifically for product photography: background removal, AI-generated backgrounds, and batch editing for solopreneurs selling physical goods without a studio. Pricing runs $7.50/month (Pro, billed yearly) to $20.99/month (Max), with a usable free tier to test the workflow first. The site's verdict is the Pro plan is one of the best-value single-purpose tools on this list, cheap, does one job well, and the free tier is enough to confirm it fits your product photos before paying.

Photoroom's core workflow is simple: drop in a product photo, and it isolates the subject, removes the background, and can generate a new one, a studio backdrop, a lifestyle scene, a solid color, in seconds, replacing what used to require a photographer or a Photoshop subscription and real editing skill.

Photoroom is purpose-built for e-commerce listings specifically, not general photo editing. It's used by resellers on Depop and Mercari, dropshippers, and small brands who need consistent, professional-looking product shots at volume without hiring a photographer for every SKU.

Pricing breakdown

  • Free: Core background removal tools, watermarked or limited exports depending on usage
  • Pro ($7.50/mo billed yearly, $12.99/mo month-to-month): Up to 8,000 AI credits, 1,000 exports/month, unlimited core tools, AI Backgrounds, AI Fashion Models
  • Max ($20.99/mo billed yearly, $34.99/mo month-to-month): Up to 25,000 AI credits, 3,000 exports/month, adds Video Generator and Adjustable AI Shadows

Why this is a genuinely good deal for solo sellers

At $7.50/month billed yearly, Photoroom Pro costs less than a single hour of a freelance photo editor's time and handles hundreds of product images a month. For a solopreneur running an online store, a reselling account, or a print-on-demand shop, that's a direct, measurable cost replacement rather than a vague productivity claim. The free tier is honest enough to test on your actual product photos before committing, which is the right way to evaluate a tool like this.

Common questions

Is Photoroom's free tier good enough to test on real product photos?

Yes, and it's worth doing before paying. Try the free tier on your actual product photos first, then upgrade to Pro if the background-removal and editing quality holds up for your specific products.

Is Photoroom worth it if I don't sell physical products?

No, it's a narrow use case built specifically for product photography. If you're not selling physical goods, there's little value here compared to a general-purpose design tool.

Is the Max plan worth the extra cost?

Usually not for most solo sellers. Max's added features like the Video Generator are overkill unless you specifically need video content alongside product photos, Pro covers the core use case.
