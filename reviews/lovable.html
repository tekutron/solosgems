Lovable generates a working application, both the visual frontend and the functional backend, from a plain-language description of what you want built, then lets you keep iterating conversationally, "add a login page", "connect this to a database", rather than writing code yourself.

Lovable's differentiator against a typical no-code builder is generating a real, functional codebase, both frontend and backend, from natural language, rather than assembling pre-built visual blocks, which produces a more genuinely custom result at the cost of more unpredictable output.

Pricing breakdown

  • Free: 5 daily credits, capped at 30 credits/month, no credit card required
  • Pro ($25/mo billed annually): 100 credits/month included, usage-based scaling for heavier projects
  • Business ($50/mo billed annually): 100 credits/month, adds SSO authentication and data training opt-out
  • Enterprise: Custom pricing for larger organizations

Where the credit system actually bites

Lovable's advertised monthly price only covers the included credit allowance, and building anything beyond a simple prototype, real database logic, multiple integrated features, iterative debugging, consumes credits fast. The honest expectation for a non-technical founder is that the $25-50/month plan price is a starting point, not a ceiling, and real projects with any complexity will likely need supplemental credit purchases on top of the base subscription.

Common questions

Can Lovable really replace hiring a developer?

For simpler apps, it's a genuinely impressive starting point for non-technical founders. For anything with real complexity, treat it as a prototype tool, not a substitute for a development team, generated code quality and reliability vary with how complex the app gets.

Will Lovable actually cost what's advertised?

Maybe not. It runs on a credit system, so real costs can climb well past the advertised monthly price if you're iterating heavily, budget some buffer above the sticker price.

Is the free plan enough to test it?

Enough to get a feel for it. The free plan includes 30 credits a month, enough to build and iterate on a simple prototype before deciding whether to pay for more capacity.
