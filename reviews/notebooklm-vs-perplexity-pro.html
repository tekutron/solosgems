NotebookLM, in short

NotebookLM is Google's source-grounded AI notebook: upload your own documents, transcripts, or notes and get answers, summaries, and audio overviews that cite back to what you actually gave it, not the open web. The free tier (100 notebooks, 50 sources each, 50 chat questions a day) is generous enough that most solopreneurs never need to pay.

Perplexity Pro, in short

Perplexity Pro is a cited, sourced AI search tool that pulls from the live open web, useful for competitive research and fact-checking your own content. It's a nice-to-have rather than a must-have if you already pay for another AI assistant with web browsing built in.

Who should pick which

If your research work is mostly digesting your own documents, client materials, meeting transcripts, past research, NotebookLM's free tier covers it without a subscription. If you need live, cited web search and don't already have that through ChatGPT or Claude, Perplexity Pro fills that specific gap, but check whether your existing AI subscription already includes browsing before paying for a second one.

Common questions

Is NotebookLM or Perplexity Pro better for a solopreneur?

They serve different purposes: NotebookLM is free and best for getting cited answers out of your own uploaded documents, while Perplexity Pro costs $16.60-$20/month and is best for live, cited web research. Most solopreneurs won't need both, but they aren't direct substitutes for each other.

Which is better for research grounded in my own documents?

NotebookLM, clearly. It's built specifically to answer questions from your own uploaded sources, client notes, transcripts, research PDFs, and deliberately won't answer outside them, which is exactly what you want for source-grounded research.

Do I need Perplexity Pro if I already have ChatGPT or Claude with browsing?

Probably not. Perplexity Pro's edge is citation quality on open-web research, but it overlaps heavily with browsing-enabled plans you may already pay for elsewhere.

Can I use both for different tasks?

Yes, and it's a reasonable split: NotebookLM for anything grounded in your own documents, Perplexity Pro or an existing browsing-enabled AI subscription for open-web research, they're not really competing for the same job.
