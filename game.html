How the storygame works

The Road to Solos Gems is a text adventure in the old choose-your-own-adventure tradition: you read a page, pick what to do, and the story branches. Your goal is to reach Solos Gems, the one shop in the realm rumored to tell you the truth about a product before you buy it. Getting there means surviving a road of tollbooths that each insist they are the last one, a billing troll, some deeply embarrassing demos, and a graveyard of AI tools that didn't make it, a few of whom you may recognize from our DeathStack.

There are no dice and no combat. What decides your fate is your character sheet, plus the choices you actually make along the way.

Build your character

Before the story starts, you spend 7 points across eight AI-flavored abilities. Every ability starts at 1, and no ability can go above 4, so you can't be great at everything. That is the point.

  • Prompt Engineering: talking your way into, or out of, almost anything.
  • Hallucination Resistance: noticing when a confident stranger is making it up.
  • Context Window: remembering what happened three scenes ago, when it suddenly matters.
  • Fine-Tuning: getting very, very good at one narrow thing.
  • Agency: acting on your own instead of waiting for permission.
  • Alignment: doing what you said you would do.
  • Retrieval: finding the right scroll in a library of wrong ones.
  • Multimodality: reading the room, the map, and the fine print at the same time.

Some choices only appear if an ability is high enough, the same way others only appear if you picked up the right item earlier. Two players with different builds will read noticeably different stories.

Twelve endings and an Exit Interview

The road has twelve endings: four ways to win and eight ways to end up somewhere else entirely, usually still paying for a tool your cousin recommended years ago. Quietly, the game also keeps an Alignment Drift score that tracks whether your choices matched the character you claimed to be. Every run closes with an Exit Interview that sums up how you played, including whether you were honest with yourself.

Saving and replaying

Your progress saves in your own browser under the name you enter, so you can pick up where you left off on the same device, keep several readers going at once, or switch between them. Nothing is sent to us. To see more of the road, start a new reader with a different build, the story opens up in ways a single run never shows.
